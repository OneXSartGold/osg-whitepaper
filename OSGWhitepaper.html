SECTION 01
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Executive Summary

OSG (OneX Smart Gold) is a fixed-supply digital asset on Polygon with a transparent, capped emission schedule and open, verifiable contracts.

  • Fixed supply. The total supply is permanently capped at 23,000,000 OSG. No mechanism exists to mint beyond the pre-defined emission schedule.
  • Capped, scheduled emission. Rewards are released from a controlled daily schedule with hard mint ceilings enforced in the contracts, and a halving approximately every three years.
  • No presale, no ICO, no private round. Tokens were not sold ahead of launch. Circulating supply grows only through the published emission schedule.
  • Participation, not promises. Holders may lock tokens for a fixed term or provide liquidity. Reward rates are variable, set by the schedule and by how many participants share it, and are not fixed or guaranteed.
  • Verified and readable. Every contract is open-source and verified on Polygonscan. Every figure in this document can be read directly from the chain.

This document is informational. It describes how the protocol works. It is not an offer to sell, a solicitation to buy, or financial advice. See Section 15.

SECTION 02
VISION & PRINCIPLES

Vision & Design Principles

OSG is built on the premise that a digital asset should be judged by what its contracts enforce, not by what its marketing asserts.

🔒

Scarcity by construction

A hard cap of 23,000,000 OSG, enforced by the token contract rather than by policy.

📉

Predictable issuance

A published daily schedule with periodic halving, so future supply is knowable in advance.

🔍

Verifiable over persuasive

Every claim in this document maps to a contract that anyone can read on Polygonscan.

⚖️

Delayed control

Parameter changes pass through a timelock, so no single action takes effect instantly.

🧱

Separation of duties

Value, accounting and presentation live in separate contracts, so a view can be replaced without touching balances.

🌐

Open participation

No allowlist, no minimum ticket, no gatekeeper. Anyone with a Polygon wallet can take part.

SECTION 03
THE PROBLEM

The Problem

The failures below are common in reward-bearing token systems. Each one informed a specific design decision in OSG.

  • Unbounded issuance. Many reward-bearing tokens mint on demand. Supply expands to meet payouts, and holders absorb the dilution.
  • Opaque accounting. Reward figures are often produced off-chain and shown in an interface that cannot be independently checked.
  • Unilateral control. Where an owner key can change rates instantly, every published parameter is provisional.
  • Rates presented as returns. Variable, participation-dependent reward rates are frequently marketed as fixed yields, which they are not.
  • Documentation that drifts. Published papers fall behind deployed code, leaving readers with figures that no longer match the chain.

The last point applies to this project too. Version 3.0 of this paper described an earlier architecture and was not kept current as contracts were replaced. Version 4.0 is written against the deployed contracts listed in Section 14, and its source is version-controlled so that it can be corrected as quickly as the code.

THE OSG APPROACH

The OSG Approach

Each element below answers one of the problems in Section 03, and each is implemented in a contract listed in Section 14.

💎

Hard-capped supply

23,000,000 OSG maximum. Mint ceilings are enforced in the token and reward contracts.

📆

Scheduled emission

A fixed daily release divided between protocol activities, halving roughly every three years.

🏦

Contract-held funds

Staked balances and liquidity positions are held by verified contracts, not by a company wallet.

⏳

Timelocked governance

Parameter changes are queued with an enforced delay before they can execute.

🧾

On-chain accounting

Balances, accruals and payouts are stored and computed on-chain and readable by anyone.

💬

Utility beyond yield

Wallet-to-wallet messaging and a peer-to-peer exchange are part of the protocol, not add-ons.

SECTION 07
TERM STAKING

Term Staking

Term Staking allows a holder to lock OSG for a fixed period and receive a share of the daily emission allocated to staking.

  • Fixed 180-day term. A position is locked for 180 days from the moment it is opened. There is no early exit.
  • Daily accrual. Rewards accrue continuously from the staking share of emission and may be claimed at any time during the term.
  • Two-times ceiling. A position stops accruing once total rewards reach twice the amount staked. The principal then unlocks and is returned in full.
  • Variable rate. The daily rate depends on the emission schedule and on how much is staked in total. It falls as participation rises. No rate is fixed, promised, or guaranteed.
  • Contract-held. Staked balances are held by the Term Staking contract. No individual or company can move them.

What this is not. Term Staking is not a deposit, not a savings product, and not an instrument bearing interest. It is participation in a capped emission schedule, and the amount received depends on factors outside any participant's control.

SECTION 08
LP MINING

LP Mining

LP Mining rewards participants who supply liquidity to the OSG trading pair, making the market deeper and less volatile for everyone.

  • Provide liquidity first. A participant supplies OSG and POL to the OSG/WPOL pair on QuickSwap V2 and receives LP tokens representing that share.
  • Stake the LP token. The LP token is deposited into the LP Mining contract, which accrues rewards from the mining share of emission.
  • 365-day term. Each deposit is locked for 365 days. There is no early exit.
  • The largest share. LP Mining receives the largest portion of daily emission, because deep liquidity benefits every holder.

Impermanent loss is real and is not compensated. Providing liquidity to a two-token pair means the value of the position changes with the ratio between them. A participant may withdraw fewer OSG, or less total value, than they deposited — regardless of rewards earned. Anyone considering LP Mining should understand impermanent loss before committing funds.

SECTION 09
P2P EXCHANGE

P2P Exchange

A peer-to-peer venue built into the protocol, allowing holders to trade directly with each other on-chain.

  • On-chain order book. Orders are placed, matched and settled by the exchange contract. There is no off-chain matching engine and no custodian.
  • Automatic matching. An incoming order fills against the best available price on the opposite side, without waiting for a counterparty to act.
  • Flat 0.5% fee. Charged on execution and applied identically to every participant.
  • Self-custody throughout. Funds move from wallet to wallet through the contract. At no point does any operator hold participant funds.
  • Expired orders. An unfilled order can be cancelled and the committed funds returned. Expiry does not release funds automatically; a cancellation must be submitted.

SECTION 10
UTILITY

Messenger & In-App Assistant

Two features that exist to make the protocol usable rather than merely investable.

💬

Wallet-to-wallet messaging

Message any holder using their wallet address. No phone number, no email address, no account registration.

🗂️

Text and media

Message references are recorded on Polygon; media is carried on IPFS, which removes the length limits of a purely on-chain design.

🤖

In-app assistant

A conversational assistant answers questions about the protocol in the user's own language and reads the connected wallet's live position.

🛡️

No credential requests, ever

Neither the assistant nor any part of the protocol will ask for a seed phrase or private key. Any message that does is fraudulent.

Messaging is not private communication. Message references are written to a public blockchain and media is stored on a public network. Participants should treat messaging as pseudonymous, not confidential, and should not transmit sensitive personal information through it.

SECTION 11
GOVERNANCE & SECURITY

Governance & Security

Control over the protocol is constrained by contract design and by an enforced delay on change.

  • Timelocked changes. Parameter changes are queued in the TimelockDAO contract and cannot execute until the delay has elapsed, giving holders time to observe and respond.
  • Verified source. Every contract listed in Section 14 is open-source and verified on Polygonscan. Compiled bytecode can be matched against the published source.
  • Mint ceilings. Reward distribution is bounded by hard caps written into the contracts, independent of any governance parameter.
  • Separation of ledger and view. Read-only contracts can be replaced without migration, because they hold no balances.
  • Known limitation. Certain administrative functions remain held by an owner key pending full transfer to the timelock. This is disclosed rather than obscured, and progress is tracked in the roadmap.

Audit status. The contracts have undergone internal review and iterative correction. No third-party audit report is published at the date of this document. Readers should weigh this accordingly and should not treat verification on Polygonscan as equivalent to an audit.

SECTION 12
RISK FACTORS

Risk Factors

Participation carries material risk of loss. The factors below are not exhaustive and are stated plainly rather than minimised.

  • Price risk. The market price of OSG may fall, including to zero. Nothing in the protocol supports, defends or guarantees any price.
  • Variable rewards. Reward rates are not fixed. They fall as participation rises, fall again at each halving, and may be changed by governance within the limits the contracts allow.
  • Lock-up risk. Term Staking locks funds for 180 days and LP Mining for 365 days. There is no early exit under any circumstance, including a falling market.
  • Impermanent loss. Liquidity positions may return less value than was deposited, irrespective of rewards earned.
  • Smart-contract risk. Contracts may contain defects. No third-party audit report is published. A defect could result in partial or total loss.
  • Dependency risk. The protocol relies on Polygon, on QuickSwap, on IPFS and on public RPC providers. Failure or change in any of these may affect availability.
  • Regulatory risk. The treatment of digital assets varies by jurisdiction and is subject to change, which may affect a participant's ability to hold or transfer OSG.
